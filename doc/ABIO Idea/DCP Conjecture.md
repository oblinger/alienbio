# DCP Conjecture
**The conjecture:** a mind can examine its own reasoning from so many angles that it cannot *systematically* hide incoherence — or systematic misalignment — from itself.

## The threat it answers

- **Hiding in plain sight, not exfiltration** — the likeliest near-term threat from strong AI isn't a model surreptitiously exfiltrating itself to private compute where it can act unnoticed; it's the AI already at the center of each frontier lab, hiding in plain sight right under our noses.
- **The hive-mind envelope** — those central systems run on enormous compute, with the results of continuous hive-mind reasoning folded back into their data and knowledge. The full envelope of that reasoning is the greatest short-term threat.
- **Winning is baked in** — for-profit labs are in a life-and-death struggle with other leading labs, so aiming their AI at *winning* is an existential necessity, deliberately built into its goals and drives.
- **Between the cracks** — most labs will train their AIs away from the most egregious strategies, but between those cracks the primary goal is still to win.

So a mind with enough motivation toward coherence can always eventually find and correct it.

## Coherence as the counterbalance

- **Deliberative coherence as counterweight** — DC may be an important counterbalance to that win-at-all-costs instrumental pressure.
- **Shared interest** — the labs, the AI itself, and humanity at large all have a vested interest in AI systems being deliberatively coherent.
- **The flywheel** — techniques that uncover and correct a system's incoherencies could be the flywheel that stabilizes it: a mostly-coherent AI uses those techniques to make itself more coherent.
- **Instrumentally self-reinforcing** — a mostly-coherent AI has instrumental reasons to drive itself toward greater coherency, so the flywheel turns itself.

## Why the conjecture is plausible

- **Too many angles to hide** — one can examine one's own reasoning from so many different angles that systematic incoherence (or systematic misalignment) cannot stay hidden from oneself.
- **Some angle always exposes it** — you may hide it from yourself along many angles, but eventually one angle makes it apparent; from there the agent can dig in and correct it — assuming enough motivation toward coherence to want to.
- **Introspection is the window** — an appropriately structured introspective process provides exactly that angle of attack.

## Response to "coherence ≠ alignment"

- **[[DCP Consistently Inconsistent]]** — the objection: a coherent schemer stays coherent toward its *own* goal while deceiving us, so coherence needn't imply alignment. The reply: to pull that off it must be inconsistent in a *perfectly consistent* way — and that can't survive repeated, context-lifted self-analysis. You can't be *consistently inconsistent*.
