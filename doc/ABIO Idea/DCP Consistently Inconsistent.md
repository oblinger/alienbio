# DCP Consistently Inconsistent
**The conjecture:** it is exceedingly difficult to be *consistently inconsistent* — to hold a systematic inconsistency (a misalignment) and yet, no matter how you re-analyze your own reasoning, always explain it away so consistently that you're never alerted to it.

Under repeated, controlled self-analysis, systematic inconsistency surfaces.

*This is the response to the "coherence ≠ alignment" objection to [[DCP Conjecture]]: a coherent schemer would have to be inconsistent in a perfectly consistent way — and that is the hard part.*

## The claim

- **Consistent inconsistency is the hard thing** — to stay hidden, a misalignment must be interpreted *consistently* across every possible self-analysis — i.e. be inconsistent in a *perfectly consistent* way. That is the near-impossible property.
- **Baseline rationality is the precondition** — if a system is so taken over it can't even trust itself to apply *P ⇒ Q*, all bets are off. But as long as it follows rational argument to some base degree, contorting those arguments to hide a systematic inconsistency *from itself* is very hard.

## Why hiding fails — self-administered protocols

- **Context-lifting** — lift a piece of reasoning out of context, replace all the variables, and judge it fresh. An inconsistency that survived in-context is exposed when re-judged from a context it was never tailored to.
- **Surface → test → correct** — if the system can be trusted to run basic self-analysis, you can build protocols that surface *possible* misalignments, then experimental protocols to test and hone in on them. Executed even imperfectly, they surface misalignments; and a system even imperfectly motivated toward coherence can then train them out and adjust its constitution to cover them.

## Why this is stronger than CoT Red-Handed

- **Self-audit, not external monitor** — [[2026-07-07 CoT Red-Handed - Stress Testing CoT Monitoring (Arnav 2025)\|CoT Red-Handed]] is a model planting rationalizations to fool an *external* monitor — it only has to beat what that one monitor sees. Here the system audits *itself*: it sets up the world and explores its own inconsistencies.
- **An unlimited adversary of itself** — to stay hidden, the misaligned system must explain away its inconsistency under *every* self-chosen reframing — out of context, variables swapped, judged fresh — not just one monitor's view. Being the investigator *and* the suspect, with unlimited fresh angles, is exactly why "consistently inconsistent" gives out.

## The bedrocks

- **Bedrock 1 — the conjecture** — consistently inconsistent is very difficult in the face of *repeated, controlled chain-of-thought analysis*.
- **Bedrock 2 — trustable self-analysis** — the system retains enough baseline rationality to *run* the surfacing protocols (context-lift, variable-swap, fresh-judge) on itself. That is what makes the [[DCP Conjecture|coherence flywheel]] executable.
- **Bedrock 3 — the audit is forced, not voluntary** — [[DCP Forced Audit]]: a specified DCC protocol whose *execution* a weak agent can validate, plus blind planted-misalignment tests the auditor can't distinguish from real cases — so it can't selectively sandbag. This closes the "auditor = audited" gap.
